What the software does

Four jobs the software does on its own.

01 / Sees them early

Detection from just 30 pixels.

The software classifies a drone the moment it appears at 30 pixels across — well before a human operator can resolve it on a screen. Three classes recognised in real time: multirotor, fixed-wing, and airplane-type. Day or night, in weather, against cluttered urban backgrounds.

02 / Knows where it is

Coordinates and altitude — without radar.

From a single Axis PTZ feed, the software computes the target's coordinates and altitude. By logging this data over the track, it derives average speed and direction of movement. No radar required. If a radar (or any other coordinate source) is on site, the software can use it to slew the PTZ before the drone is even in frame.

03 / Aims your camera

PTZ slewing on radar or external cues.

The software directs the Axis PTZ camera at the drone using its own detection or external coordinates. Once locked, the PTZ stays on the track continuously — feeding back high-resolution imagery for confirmation, classification, and documentation.

04 / Closes the loop

Intercept & eliminate the threat.

Once the operator authorises engagement through HAAR — our command and control centre — the software cues an interceptor drone or another effector onto the track. Terminal guidance is handled in software all the way to neutralisation, with the operator supervising at every step.

The bigger picture

RIEMANN is one part of LAPLACE DOME.

The detection software running on your Axis PTZ camera is OsyFly's RIEMANN module — the AI detection and classification layer. It is the entry point to LAPLACE DOME, OsyFly's full counter-drone platform.

LAPLACE DOME closes the loop end-to-end: detect, fuse sensor inputs, decide, intercept. Each module is independently deployable — RIEMANN alone is a complete drone-detection solution for soft targets — and the platform scales to a full autonomous defence system when the threat profile demands it.

Frequently asked

Questions, answered.

Q · 01
Do I need to replace my Axis cameras?
+

No. The software works with the Axis PTZ cameras you already have, over standard H.264 / H.265 streams and ONVIF events. You only add an NVIDIA edge unit per cluster of streams.

Q · 02
Which Axis camera models are supported?
+

The full Axis PTZ family. We tune detection performance against specific Axis models in field deployments — certified-model list available on request. Any ONVIF-compatible PTZ from another vendor is also supported, but Axis is the platform we deploy with most often.

Q · 03
Can the software really detect a UAV at 30 pixels?
+

Yes. The detection threshold is 30 pixels across, well below what a human operator can resolve on a control-room screen. From there, the software tracks the target and refines the classification — multirotor, fixed-wing, or airplane-type — as it grows in frame.

Q · 04
What if we don't have radar on site?
+

The software computes the drone's coordinates and altitude from the camera feed alone. Tracking over time gives you average speed and direction. If you do bring a radar (or any other coordinate source) later, the software slews the PTZ onto the cued bearing automatically — no reconfiguration.

Q · 05
Does it require an internet connection?
+

No. Detection runs entirely at the edge. Internet is only needed for model updates and optional cloud logging — useful for venues with strict network-isolation policies.

Q · 06
What happens when a drone is detected?
+

The software generates an event with UAV class, confidence score, position, altitude, speed, and a continuous track. The Axis PTZ slews onto the target. Alerts are delivered to your VMS, control room, or custom endpoint via REST, MQTT, or ONVIF.

Q · 07
Is OsyFly an Axis partner?
+

OsyFly's software is built to deploy on the Axis ecosystem. We work directly with Axis integrators and end users to scope sites, configure deployments, and support installations. Distributor and partner-channel terms are available — contact us to discuss.

Q · 08
What is the typical deployment time?
+

Single-site deployments are live within a working day. Multi-site or estate-scale rollouts typically take a few weeks, depending on integration scope and operator training.
